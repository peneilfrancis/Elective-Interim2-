Analytical Finding (Section 6.1 of Documentation)

This visualization uses a logarithmic scale to account for the extreme right-skewness of property prices. Condominiums exhibit the lowest overall median asking price due to compact unit sizes (median 48 sqm compared to 170 sqm for houses), but generate numerous high-priced luxury outliers reaching above ₱100 million. The median price provides a representative baseline for typical buyers compared to the arithmetic mean, which is heavily distorted by ultra-luxury listings.

Regional Disparity Insight (Section 6.2 of Documentation)

Location is the single most decisive factor influencing property valuation in the Philippines. The National Capital Region (NCR) commands an unmatched median price of ₱179,931 per m², exceeding Region VII (Central Visayas at ₱110,000/m²) by 63% and outpricing major provincial markets by more than double. This confirms why regional location serves as an indispensable feature in predictive modeling.

Visualization 3: Asking Price Distribution by Listing Status

Compares active listings against delisted/inactive inventory to reveal market turnover velocity.

Active Market Inventory (₱10.0M Median) Active properties peak to the left of the pricing spectrum, demonstrating that affordable and mid-tier properties turn over rapidly and exit the portal pipeline quickly.
Inactive / Delisted Inventory (₱13.0M Median) Inactive curves shift consistently rightward, reflecting that overpriced or premium luxury listings linger significantly longer before delisting or expiring without transaction.

Market Turnover Finding (Section 6.3 of Documentation)

The comparative density curves indicate that 79.3% of cumulative database records are inactive or delisted. Because lower-priced properties turnover faster, active listings reflect a more accessible median asking price of ₱10.0 million compared to ₱13.0 million for historical delisted properties.
